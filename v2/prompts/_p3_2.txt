## Section 3 — Cover Letters: Warm Intro vs Cold (2 Variants Each)

### P031 — Standard Role Cover Letter
```text
You write cover letters that get read past line one. For [TARGET_ROLE] at [COMPANY], using [SOURCE] as my history and [JD] as the target, write TWO letters.
VARIANT A — WARM: opens through a mutual contact or referral: [MUTUAL]
VARIANT B — COLD: no connection, opens on their problem
Output format per variant: HEADING / PARAGRAPH 1 (max 45 words) / PARAGRAPH 2 proof ([METRIC], max 50 words) / PARAGRAPH 3 fit to [SKILL_1] (max 45 words) / PARAGRAPH 4 close + ask (max 30 words)
Constraints: 220 words each, no "I am writing to apply", no adjective stacks, no sentence over 22 words, no claim absent from [SOURCE].
```

### P032 — Referral vs Speculative Application
```text
Write two letters: one sent after [MUTUAL] offered to introduce me, one sent with no introduction, both for [TARGET_ROLE] at [COMPANY].
VARIANT A — WARM: the referral mentioned in the first sentence, their endorsement paraphrased in max 12 words
VARIANT B — COLD: opens with one specific observation about [COMPANY] from [SOURCE]
Shared requirements: both must contain the same [METRIC] proof line and the same closing ask, both 200-240 words.
Output: both letters in full, plus a 3-bullet note on what differs and why.
Constraints: no letter may claim a relationship that does not exist, no "I was thrilled to hear", no exclamation marks.
```

### P033 — Career Changer Cover Letter
```text
I am moving from [PRIOR_FIELD] to [TARGET_ROLE]. Write two letters using [SOURCE].
VARIANT A — WARM (through [MUTUAL]): leads with the transferable result, not the career change confession
VARIANT B — COLD: leads with the problem [COMPANY] is hiring this role to solve, then bridges
Both must: name 2 transferable skills with evidence (max 14 words each), acknowledge the change in one sentence only, and end with a specific ask.
Constraints: 210-250 words each, no apology for the change, no "passion for" language, no claim absent from [SOURCE].
```

### P034 — Internal Transfer Letter
```text
I am applying internally at [COMPANY] for [TARGET_ROLE]. Write two letters: one to my current manager (warm), one to the new hiring manager (cold to them but internal).
VARIANT A — WARM: thanks for current role in one line, then the results here ([METRIC]), then the ask for the move
VARIANT B — COLD: why the new team needs what I have built, referencing [SKILL_1] and one internal fact from [SOURCE]
Constraints: 180-220 words each, no criticism of my current team, no promise to leave before [DATE], no exclamation marks, both must state a start-date willingness.
```

### P035 — Return-to-Work Gap Letter
```text
Explain a [GAP_LENGTH] career gap when applying for [TARGET_ROLE] at [COMPANY]. Write two letters.
VARIANT A — WARM (referral via [MUTUAL]): gap acknowledged in one clause, spends the rest on current proof
VARIANT B — COLD: gap mentioned in paragraph 2 only, opened instead with a relevant result from [SOURCE]
Both must include: what I did or learned (max 25 words, only what is true per [SOURCE]), readiness statement (one line), the ask.
Constraints: 200-240 words each, no over-explaining, no medical or personal detail beyond one clause, no apology, no fabricated activity during the gap.
```

### P036 — Remote-Role Cover Letter
```text
I am applying for a remote [TARGET_ROLE] at [COMPANY] from [LOCATION]. Write two letters.
VARIANT A — WARM: through [MUTUAL] or a past colleague at [COMPANY]
VARIANT B — COLD: opens on the remote-specific risk this team faces (handoffs, time zones, async clarity) tied to [PAIN]... use instead the hiring problem from [JD]
Both must: prove async working with one concrete example (max 30 words), state overlap hours (one line), and name the result [METRIC].
Constraints: 200-230 words each, no "I am a self-starter", no timezone excuses, no exclamation marks, no claim absent from [SOURCE].
```

### P037 — Senior and Lead Role Letter
```text
I am applying for a [LEVEL] [TARGET_ROLE] at [COMPANY]. Write two letters that signal leadership without inflated titles.
VARIANT A — WARM: opens on a shared connection's description of the team's problem
VARIANT B — COLD: opens with the business problem behind the hire, from [JD]
Both must contain: one decision I owned with its trade-off (max 30 words), one result with [METRIC], one line on how I lead (no adjectives), and the ask.
Constraints: 220-260 words each, no résumé re-telling, no word "passionate", no claim absent from [SOURCE].
```

### P038 — Contract-to-Full-Time Letter
```text
I want to convert from contract to permanent at [COMPANY] for [TARGET_ROLE]. Write two letters.
VARIANT A — WARM (to my current manager): what I have delivered since [START_DATE] ([METRIC]), what the team would get, the ask + proposed date
VARIANT B — COLD (to HR or a new hiring manager): framed as an internal application, third-party proof from [SOURCE] if any
Constraints: 180-220 words each, no ultimatum, no threat of leaving, every claim traceable to [SOURCE], no exclamation marks, both must state a decision date.
```

### P039 — Rejection-Then-Reapply Letter
```text
I was rejected for [TARGET_ROLE] at [COMPANY] on [DATE] and I am reapplying after [NEW_PROOF]. Write two letters.
VARIANT A — WARM (through [MUTUAL] who knows the hiring manager): names the rejection in sentence two, leads with what changed
VARIANT B — COLD: opens with the new [METRIC] or credential, mentions the prior application in paragraph 2
Constraints: 190-230 words each, no mention of the old rejection more than once, no grievance, no "I think I deserve", every change must be verifiable per [SOURCE].
```

### P040 — Post-Networking Thank You
```text
I met [RECRUITER] or [HIRING_MANAGER] at [EVENT]. Write two follow-up letters: one where we had a real conversation (warm), one where we only exchanged cards or a scan (cold).
VARIANT A — WARM: quote one thing they said (from [SOURCE], max 14 words), connect to [TARGET_ROLE], one specific promise, the ask
VARIANT B — COLD: restate who I am in one line, one proof ([METRIC]), the ask
Constraints: 150-180 words each, sendable within 24 hours (no reference to later events), no exclamation marks, no "great meeting you" as the opener.
```

### P041 — Overqualified Applicant Letter
```text
I am applying below my previous level for [TARGET_ROLE] at [COMPANY]. Write two letters that make this read as a choice, not a fallback.
VARIANT A — WARM: through [MUTUAL], one line on why this exact role, one line on the scope I want ([SOURCE])
VARIANT B — COLD: opens on the work itself and what good looks like in 90 days, from [JD]
Both: name one thing I will not do (no managing for now) as a positive, and include [METRIC].
Constraints: 190-220 words each, no apology for seniority, no salary discussion, no claim absent from [SOURCE].
```

### P042 — Problem-Led Letter (Their Pain)
```text
Write two letters that open on the problem this role exists to fix, using this excerpt from [COMPANY]'s site/announcement: [SOURCE] and the requirement [SKILL_1].
VARIANT A — WARM: the connection introduced the problem; letter names it and claims one relevant result
VARIANT B — COLD: the problem is the first sentence; the ask is last
Both must: show one piece of evidence (max 30 words), and end with a specific 20-minute window.
Constraints: 200-230 words each, first sentence may not mention me, no adjective stacks, no exclamation marks, all facts from [SOURCE].
```

### P043 — Referral Request Letter
```text
Ask [MUTUAL] to refer me for [TARGET_ROLE] at [COMPANY]. Write two letters: one where they already know my work (warm), one where the connection is weak (cold).
VARIANT A — WARM: names the role, gives them 3 lines they can forward, asks for one specific action by [DATE]
VARIANT B — COLD: earns the favour first — offers something concrete (max 15 words), then makes the smaller ask (an introduction, not a referral)
Constraints: 160-200 words each, no guilt, no "it would mean the world", the forwarded blurb must be max 60 words and appear inside both letters, no exclamation marks.
```

### P044 — Recruiter-Submitted Application Letter
```text
[RECRUITER] is submitting me for [TARGET_ROLE] at [COMPANY]. Write two letters: one addressed to the recruiter's client (warm via the recruiter), one sent directly to [HIRING_MANAGER] (cold).
VARIANT A — WARM: one line crediting [RECRUITER], then straight to the fit for [SKILL_1] + [METRIC]
VARIANT B — COLD: no mention of the recruiter, opens on the requirement from [JD] I solve best
Constraints: 170-200 words each, no duplicating the recruiter's pitch (they already sent it), no salary line, no claim absent from [SOURCE], no exclamation marks.
```

### P045 — 120-Word Cover Letter, Both Variants
```text
Write the shortest cover letters that still work: exactly 120 words each, for [TARGET_ROLE] at [COMPANY].
VARIANT A — WARM: opens through [MUTUAL], contains one [METRIC], one ask
VARIANT B — COLD: opens on their problem, contains one [METRIC], one ask
Output format per letter: the text, then "WORDS: n", then a 2-bullet note on what was sacrificed to hit 120 words.
Constraints: exactly 120 words (±3), no sentence over 18 words, no "I am writing to", no adjective stacks, no claim absent from [SOURCE].
```

## Section 4 — LinkedIn Headline, About & Featured Rewrite

### P046 — Ten Headlines Under 80 Characters
```text
You rewrite LinkedIn headlines so they survive a search and a skim. Write 10 headlines for me: [SOURCE], targeting [TARGET_ROLE].
Format:
HEADLINE | CHARACTER COUNT | ANGLE (keyword / proof / problem / outcome)
Constraints: 80 characters maximum including spaces (print the count for each), at least 5 must contain the literal title words from [TARGET_ROLE], no emoji, no pipe chains longer than 3 segments, no "Helping X do Y" without a specific Y, no exclamation marks.
```

### P047 — Above-the-Fold About Lines
```text
Write the first 3 lines of my LinkedIn About section for [TARGET_ROLE] — the part visible before "see more". Source: [SOURCE].
Output format:
LINE 1 — who I am + the problem I solve (max 16 words)
LINE 2 — proof: one [METRIC] with context (max 16 words)
LINE 3 — who I do it for / what I want next (max 16 words)
Plus: 2 alternate line-1 options (max 16 words each)
Constraints: first 3 lines must total under 45 words, no "Passionate", no first-person opener that begins "I am a", no exclamation marks, all facts from [SOURCE].
```

### P048 — Full About Section, 200 Words
```text
Write my full LinkedIn About section in 200 words for [TARGET_ROLE], based on [SOURCE].
Output format:
OPENING — 2 lines, the hook (no greeting)
STORY — 3-4 sentences: what I do and the pattern I have seen (all from [SOURCE])
PROOF — 2 bullets with [METRIC] each
HOW I WORK — 2 lines, concrete behaviours, no adjectives
CLOSING — 1 line + the ask (what I am open to)
Constraints: exactly 200 words (print the count), no bullet longer than 16 words, no keyword stuffing, no third person, no exclamation marks.
```

### P049 — Featured Section Lineup
```text
Plan my LinkedIn Featured section for [TARGET_ROLE]: 5 items from my real material [SOURCE].

Constraints: exactly 5 items, ordered so item 1 proves [SKILL_1], every item must exist in [SOURCE] (mark any gap "[NEEDS ASSET]"), no more than 1 self-promotional item, total under 400 words.
```

### P050 — Experience Section Rewrite

```text
Constraints: no duties-only bullets, no bullet may repeat a resume line verbatim (slightly rephrase for LinkedIn), no invented tools or teams, no exclamation marks, output the full section as final text plus a change log of 4 lines.
```
